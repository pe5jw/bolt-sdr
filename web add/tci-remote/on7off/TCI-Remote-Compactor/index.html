The problem

Raw TCI traffic is brutally hungry on bandwidth

Thetis outputs uncompressed audio and a full waterfall stream — easily 2 Mbps or more. That is fine on a gigabit LAN, but completely unusable over a mobile data connection. Without a solution, remote operation simply does not work.

📡

4G/5G drops constantly

Raw TCI saturates a typical mobile data plan within minutes. Audio breaks up, the waterfall freezes, the app disconnects.

🔌

Port forwarding is a nightmare

Most routers, ISPs, and 5G home gateways block incoming connections. Even when it works, your home IP changes without warning.

🔓

No access control in Thetis

Opening Thetis to the internet means anyone who finds your IP can connect. There is no built-in password system.

👤

Only one user at a time

You cannot share your radio with a fellow operator or monitor it from two devices simultaneously without dropping connections.

🔥

Stuck transmitter — no recovery

TCI has no built-in disconnect handling. A dropped remote connection leaves PTT active and the tuner running — indefinitely. Burned finals, a seized ATU motor, and a destroyed PA are the result.

Opus compression engine

95% bandwidth reduction — crystal-clear audio

The Compactor intercepts the raw Thetis audio stream and re-encodes it in real time using the Opus codec — the same algorithm used by Discord, Teams, and WhatsApp. You choose the bitrate: from a razor-thin 8 kbps on a weak connection up to 320 kbps for studio-quality monitoring.

Equipment protection

One dropped connection can destroy your radio gear

The TCI protocol has no built-in disconnect handling. Thetis cannot tell the difference between a deliberate stop and a crashed connection. If you drop off mid-transmission — dead zone, crashed app, sudden network loss — Thetis keeps transmitting, keeps tuning, keeps running the ATU. Indefinitely. Without a watchdog, this is how finals burn and ATU relays get destroyed.

Changelog

What's new in v1.3.4.3 — WAD off the leash, and the right sideband

Two things about WAD, and both of them are things that were in your way. You no longer have to lock the VFO before you can use it, and on the lower bands the receivers now really listen in LSB. Everything from v1.3.4.2 is included — installing this over your current version is all you have to do.

🔓

WAD Without the Lock Button

WAD used to demand that you switch the VFO lock on first, because it had no way of keeping up with a frequency that moved under it. It follows the VFO now instead: turn the knob and the receivers are retuned once you stop, each one staying out of the audio until it has confirmed the new frequency itself. So you tune the way you always tune, and the lock button goes back to being yours.

New in v1.3.4.3
📻

LSB on the WAD Receivers

On 160, 80 and 40 your radio is in LSB, but the WAD receivers kept listening in USB — the wrong sideband, so voices came back unintelligible and whatever sat on the other side of the carrier came through with them. The Compactor did send the mode; what it did not send was a passband with the sign that belongs to it, and an uberSDR applies the passband literally. It now sends the passband your radio actually has, sign and all, and AM, FM and CW get a symmetrical window instead of a single-sideband one. Measured on real receivers before and after, on both sides of the carrier.

Fixed in v1.3.4.3

Previously — v1.3.4.2

🛰

WAD — Wide Area Diversity

You know the situation: a station is working everyone and you hear nothing but noise. That is not your radio being bad, it is where your antenna happens to stand. A receiver 800 km away sits on a different hop and may hear that station perfectly.

Switch WAD on and the Compactor connects to a number of public uberSDR receivers on your frequency, next to your own radio. It measures the signal-to-noise on each of them twice a second and hands you the one that is hearing best — your own radio included, and it goes back to your own radio the moment that is the better one. The changeover is a short crossfade, so you hear a change of background noise rather than a click.

In the app you say how far away a receiver may be (a lower and an upper limit, up to 10 000 km) and how many to use at once, from three up to eight. Leave it on AUTO and it picks for you; switch to MANUAL and you tap the receiver you want to stay on, while all the others keep measuring so you can see what you are choosing between. USB, LSB, CW, AM, FM and the digital modes all follow the mode of your radio.

What it is not. WAD is receive only — you still transmit with your own radio, and WAD steps aside for as long as you are transmitting. The receivers follow your VFO, so after a band change you hear your own radio for about a second while they catch up. It needs an internet connection at the Compactor, and it depends on public receivers being online and having a free slot: every receiver you use occupies a place at somebody else's station that nobody else can use while you have it. That is exactly why the ceiling is eight and not more. The waterfall stays your own radio's — WAD is audio.

Available to the master user and to guests with TX rights. You need the TCI Remote app version that has the 🛰 button; hold it for two seconds to open the settings.
